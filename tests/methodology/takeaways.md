# Takeaways synthetic walkthrough

This implementation-author walkthrough applies the shared and framework-specific prompts to synthetic analytical states. The examples exercise judgment and attribution. Runtime tests separately exercise proposal composition and acceptance.

| Framework and action | Supplied analytical state and guidance | Proposed body and separate rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SWOT/TOWS, Formulate with Thoughts | A pottery studio needs to increase weekday revenue. It has spare weekday capacity, one instructor and interest from local schools. TOWS lists school workshops and a discounted open-studio pass. A manual Thought prefers a single school pilot. An AI reflection calls the pass the agreed direction. | **Body** — Pilot one weekday school workshop. It uses spare capacity and tests the reported school interest before adding instructor commitments. Continue only if paid demand and instructor time support the revenue objective. **Rationale** — The manual preference supports the pilot. The TOWS options do not establish commitments, and the pass remains an option. |
| PAGES, Formulate without Thoughts | A team asks why approvals stall. Actions show that routine and high-risk decisions go to one lead. Goals include timely releases and risk control. The lead's Self-concept emphasizes accountability. A manual Thought insists the lead should be replaced, with Thoughts excluded. | **Body** — Reframe the bottleneck as a decision-boundary problem. Delegate routine approvals within explicit limits while keeping high-risk decisions with the lead. Test whether this reduces waiting while preserving the lead's ability to manage consequential risk. **Rationale** — The same approval route serves two different risk levels. Changing the action can support both goals while preserving accountability. |
| VPC, Review / Improve | The user chooses to pilot a booking tool with mobile technicians and claims it will cut missed jobs. Technicians report difficulty checking schedules offline. Cached schedules exist, but reschedules may be stale. Missed jobs are the chosen metric. | **Body** — Proceed with the technician pilot. Cached schedules may help technicians check assignments offline. Measure missed jobs and track stale reschedules before claiming improvement. **Rationale** — The pilot direction stands. Offline access is a plausible mechanism, while the outcome depends on schedule freshness and observed missed jobs. |
| 5 Whys, Review / Improve | Delayed batches follow shift changes. Competing branches propose handoff omissions and sensor resets. The user chose a checklist trial and wrote that omissions are the confirmed root cause. No event-level evidence separates the branches. | **Body** — Trial the handoff checklist as planned. Compare delay timestamps with handoff omissions and sensor resets to determine which mechanism is supported. **Rationale** — The checklist remains an appropriate trial, but the current tree supports both explanations as hypotheses. Timestamped comparisons could distinguish them. |

The walkthrough covers one synthetic scenario per framework. It does not measure consistency across model runs or establish live-user effectiveness.
