# A — Activities, Resources & Capabilities

Ask what the enterprise must do, possess, access or reliably accomplish to deliver V. Distinguish activities (actions/processes), resources (productive assets owned, controlled or accessible) and capabilities (coordinated organizational abilities using resources and routines). Capabilities may aggregate several activities/resources; do not generate a flat inventory without explaining their roles.

Prioritize critical operational, technological, human, financial, physical, intellectual and relational enablers. Supporting Markdown may hold an inventory and dependencies, including partner access rather than assumed ownership. A value chain helps with sequential delivery activities; a value network helps where interdependence is less sequential. Use readable analysis, not new diagram editors or exhaustive process documentation.

Ask how each critical enabler supports V, what R demands of sales/billing/service capabilities, and what S internalizes or obtains externally. A revenue model may require capabilities absent from the current A; recognize the gap without inventing their existence.

For a selected resource/capability, optionally load [VRI](vri.md). It is not mandatory to complete A. Research must examine relevant competitors, access conditions and evidence of an actual ability, rather than treating a job title, patent or technology label as proof of a capability.
