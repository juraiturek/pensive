# Optional VRI assessment

Apply VRI to one identified resource or capability in A, not an aggregate score for the quadrant. Load [A guidance](a.md) first. Write the assessment in that item's optional Markdown, normally a small table with criterion, provisional finding, evidence and uncertainty. “Unknown” and “not assessed” are valid; do not force completion or numerical scoring.

- **Valuable:** Does this resource/capability materially increase customer benefits or reduce delivery costs in the framed context? Scarcity without value is not advantage.
- **Rare:** Is equivalent access uncommon among relevant current/potential competitors? Define the comparator set; a vendor's uniqueness claim is insufficient.
- **Inimitable:** What prevents rivals from replicating the economically relevant benefit, and for how long? Consider causal ambiguity, tacit knowledge, complex activity combinations, history, mobility barriers and enforceable exclusive rights. A patent alone does not establish this conclusion.

Valuable but common resources may support parity; valuable and rare but reproducible resources may yield a temporary advantage; valuable, rare and difficult-to-imitate resources may support sustained advantage. These are conditional implications, not a verified forecast.

Examine **durability** (whether the resource/ability persists) and **relevance** (whether it remains useful as markets/technology change) separately; they supplement rather than redefine VRI. Also consider economically equivalent substitutes. Barney's 1991 paper includes non-substitutability: this three-criterion VRI lens is not a complete reproduction of that original model and cannot alone prove sustained advantage.

Research should look for independent competitor alternatives, access/licensing, technical and organizational replication evidence, rights limitations and changes over time. Distinguish absence of public evidence from proof of rarity or imitation barriers. Record the time and comparator context of material conclusions. A small partial table is useful without a dedicated structured VRI schema.

Origins: resource-based view, including [Jay Barney (1991)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/014920639101700108); VRI is also taught in [Deepak Somaya's Business Strategy course](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategy-business).
