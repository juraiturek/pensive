# Six Hats on 5 Whys

Read the symptom, expected condition, system and investigation ownership. A child is a possible direct cause of its parent. Siblings may compete or coexist. Closure with a countermeasure is a user decision. For a node target, interpret it in its ancestral chain and use relevant siblings or descendants as context.

## White

Separate the observed symptom from reported conditions and inferred causes. Identify what evidence could distinguish competing explanations for the same parent.

## Red

Notice where wording, blame or apparent closure may invite defensiveness, unease or relief. Keep the impression separate from the causal chain.

## Black

Challenge a weak child-to-parent link through timing, mechanism or a counterexample. On a closed branch, consider what the countermeasure could leave exposed.

## Yellow

Look for exceptions where the expected condition already holds, protective conditions on adjacent branches, or a plausible leverage point in the system.

## Green

Consider a distinct mechanism, a missing intermediate or an upstream alternative. Mark it as a hypothesis and identify the link it would change.

## Blue

Locate where the investigation is stuck or losing focus. Distinguish investigating an existing link, comparing siblings and revisiting the symptom boundary.
