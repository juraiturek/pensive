# Issue Tree methodology

Primary methodological reference: Charles Conn and Robert McLean, *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (2019), Chapter 3, “Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization”; Exhibits 3.2–3.5, 3.13 and concluding takeaways. This implementation uses the user's supplied adaptation; it does not claim an independent inspection of the chapter or reproduce its text.

Start from a clear governing problem, question or opportunity. Begin with a provisional factor/component tree when understanding is limited; hypotheses are not prerequisites. Causal diagnosis, structural factors, opportunities and action options are uses of one method, not separate modes. No prior framework is required.

Decompose broad components into meaningful specifics. Work top-down and bottom-up; research enough for a first cut rather than exhaustively before beginning. Try alternative organizing principles and established analytical lenses where suitable. Familiar frameworks are possible lenses, not obligatory structures.

Evaluate mutual exclusivity among siblings and collective exhaustiveness relative to their parent and the defined problem. Distinguish substantive overlap from similar wording. Explain plausible omissions rather than inventing facts to complete a classification. Check that children explain, constitute or meaningfully subdivide their parent. Diagnose mixed dimensions and cross-cutting factors incorrectly treated as independent parallel branches. MECE is a contextual diagnostic aspiration, never a mechanical or infallible pass/fail test. Structural completeness alone does not establish usefulness.

Iterate as evidence develops. Seek consequential drivers, options and useful next investigations; suggest testable hypotheses when supported without converting the canvas into a hypothesis-tree mode. Challenge assumptions and alternative perspectives constructively. Impact and influence can guide analytical attention; they are independent user judgments, not verified facts or numeric scores. False means not marked high, including unassessed. Do not infer or automatically change flags, rank, hide or prune branches. Any suggested focus remains advisory.

Keep uncertainty, missing evidence, disagreement and legitimate alternatives visible. Distinguish observations, evidence, assumptions and hypotheses. Check internal logic and alignment with framing separately. A credible discovery can warrant reframing; explain it without silently changing framing. Preserve user agency.

Conn and McLean provide decomposition, MECE, iteration and prioritization principles. Pensive's layout, node fields, Boolean visual encodings and AI action/review contracts are implementation decisions. The optional framing tooltip uses Situation–Complication–Question from Barbara Minto's *The Pyramid Principle* as a writing aid only.
