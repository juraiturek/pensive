# Bargaining power of buyers

- Which buyer groups differ in concentration, purchasing volume and leverage relative to sellers?
- Can buyers compare undifferentiated offers, change providers or credibly integrate backward?
- Does partial internal production reveal supplier costs or improve negotiating options?
- How do switching costs and access to information affect bargaining leverage?
- How important is this purchase to buyer costs, quality and performance? Do low buyer margins increase price sensitivity?
- Are intermediaries or channels influencing downstream choices?

Separate leverage from incentives to exercise it. Large buyers are not automatically powerful; explain the mechanism. Differences between buyer groups may justify qualified assessments or reconsidering the boundary.

## Use these questions selectively

This catalogue guides manual analysis, AI interpretation, host Research and quality evaluation. It is not a required questionnaire or scoring formula. Explain observations → structural mechanism → competitive implication → force judgment, considering counterevidence. Confidence reflects relevant coverage, evidence reliability, causal specificity and unresolved assumptions; high coverage alone does not justify high confidence.

For structural trajectory, identify changing drivers, temporary/cyclical versus structural effects, a meaningful horizon, assumptions, confidence and implications. Direction refers to competitive pressure, not attractiveness. Unknown direction remains null; an assessed but indeterminate outlook is uncertain, never stable by default.

Source hierarchy: Porter (2008) establishes the mechanisms; the supplied Assen/van den Berg/Pietersma chapter summary supplements operational questions. See [methodology](../methodology.md) for provenance and boundaries.
