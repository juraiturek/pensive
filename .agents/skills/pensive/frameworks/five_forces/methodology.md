# Five Forces method and provenance

Porter’s 2008 *The Five Competitive Forces That Shape Strategy* is primary. The supplied methodological reference summarizes Assen, van den Berg and Pietersma, *Key Management Models*, chapter 4, pp. 14–18, as supplementary diagnostic guidance. The book edition/year and original pages have not been independently verified. Primary article consulted: [HBR reprint](https://public.dhe.ibm.com/software/data/sw-library/cognos/pdfs/articles/art_the_five_competitive_forces_that_shape_strategy.pdf). These are paraphrased methodological instructions, not a numerical scoring procedure.

## Iterative lifecycle

1. Framing: define industry/product-service boundaries and geography, aligned with the strategic question. Check overly broad or narrow scope. Identify initial rivals, buyer groups, supplier groups, substitutes and plausible entrants; exhaustive lists are unnecessary.
2. Canvas: investigate underlying drivers, explain mechanisms and judge force strength with evidence and uncertainty. Prioritize controlling forces rather than giving all five equal attention.
3. Canvas and Takeaways: test internal consistency and external fit, including available long-run industry economics; examine changing structure and distinguish cycles from lasting change.
4. Takeaways: explore positioning, exploiting structural change and influencing industry structure, including unintended consequences and unresolved questions.

Findings can challenge framing; synthesis can reveal missing investigation. Distinguish accidental content drift, a credible framing challenge and ambiguous intent. Propose the issue, reasons and affected conclusions. Do not redefine industry or participants automatically. The user explicitly edits framing and requests reassessment; existing content remains preserved and provisional.

Five Forces concerns industry structure and value capture through prices, costs and investment requirements, not simply a firm’s strengths or success. Company-specific profitability can differ; available economic evidence is a consistency test, not a forced equal weighting or mechanical attractiveness score. Technology, government policy, growth and complements affect structural mechanisms; do not add a sixth force.

## Pensive design choices

Strength is null or very_weak/weak/moderate/strong/very_strong; confidence is null or a finite 0–1 judgment shown as a percentage, not a calibrated statistical probability. Analysis uses stable `{id,text}` snippets; Markdown research_notes retain supporting evidence and citations separately. Explain uncertainty and confidence in analysis. No persistent coverage score or confidence formula.

Trajectory uses nullable increasing/decreasing/stable/uncertain direction, text horizon, independent confidence, analysis snippets including assumptions, and separate notes. Increasing means stronger pressure. The five-level scale, numeric confidence, record layout, arrows and T1–T4 are Pensive choices, not prescriptions by Porter or Assen.
