# Internal fit and framing–content fit

**Internal fit** concerns relationships among analytical content nodes: alignment, complementarity, causal/logical links, contradictions, tensions, or meaningful balance/completeness as defined by the framework. Do not force harmony, symmetry or agreement; exposing tension can be the useful result.

**External fit** concerns framing nodes in relation to analytical content. Both types can matter during creation and evaluation, but their criteria belong to the framework. Neither automatically warrants a score or visual indicator. Review meaningful analytical tensions rather than superficial consistency. No generic fit engine is implied.

Framing defines the perspective, scope and constraints; content develops the analysis within it. During creation use available context → extract framework-defined framing → clarify essential gaps → generate methodological content → check coherence. Do not generate lists first and reverse-engineer framing to justify them. Do not add new framing fields merely because context contains information.

Distinguish explicit user statements, reasonable inferences and unresolved assumptions. Unknown optional fields need not block a useful draft. Use the selected framework's creation method to decide what is essential and when to elicit or offer a sparse draft.

During analysis, a discrepancy is an analytical signal, not automatically invalid data. Consider three explanations:

- Content drifts outside the intended scope: revise or omit that content when authorized.
- Credible content challenges the scope: surface the possibility that framing should change and explain the evidence.
- Intent or evidence is ambiguous: identify the uncertainty and ask a focused question when necessary.

Never silently redefine framing to accommodate generated content, or assume framing is correct and content wrong. A framing-preserving action may diagnose a needed reframing without applying it. Users can edit framing locally and request a fresh analysis. Deterministic validation enforces schema and action boundaries; reasoning assesses semantic coherence. No automatic synchronization or dependency graph is implied.
